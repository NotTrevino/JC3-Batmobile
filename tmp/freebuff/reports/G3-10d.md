# G3-10d — constraint partner, and the 24 packed inertia bytes

STATUS: **split.** The constraint partner is decoded far enough to name it, with a geometric
oracle and two controls; the 24-byte inertia block is **not** decoded and is recorded as UNKNOWN
with every candidate reading scored, exactly as the card allows.

- **Partner (derived).** The 24-byte cinfo is `{constraintData*, bodyIdA, bodyIdB, 0x7FFFFFFF, 0, 0}`
  fleet-wide: word `+0x04` is **0 in all 494 records**, word `+0x08` is a valid body index in all
  494. Read as a pair, the partner is **body 0 (the chassis)**. Oracle: both bodies must touch the
  constraint pivot, so the pivot's distance to each body's convex hull must be small.
  `(0, cinfo body)` passes at **35.1 %** within 5 cm against **0.53 %** for a shuffled partner
  (**66×**), and body 0 sits within 5 cm of the pivot **5×** more often than the next fixed body
  (48.4 % vs 7.2 / 6.4 / 9.7 % for bodies 1/2/3). Labelled `derived`, not `decoded`: half the pivots
  lie *inside* the chassis hull, so the oracle has a floor, and a constant 0 cannot be told from a
  constant flag by these bytes alone.
- **Frames A/B add nothing (decoded).** In the donor all **11/11** constraintData objects carry two
  identical frames: bit-identical translations, rotations agreeing to **≤ 0.05°**, every delta a
  proper rotation (det 1). The pair is one frame written twice, so the constraintData gives the
  joint's position and axis but not which body is which — that is why the hull test above is the
  oracle rather than a pivot comparison. Frame counts per constraint: 2 or 3 (ragdolls add a third).
- **Inertia (UNKNOWN).** The mass link is reproduced at fleet scale — **2011** hulls across 46 files
  (G3-10c: 54 in the donor) — and mass is the IEEE float at `+0x28`. The 24 bytes at `+0x10..+0x27`
  were tested as 6 × f32, 12 × int16 (raw, /16384, /32767), 12 × u16 and 12 × IEEE half, each scored
  by the only oracle the file supplies: `Ixx = m(ey²+ez²)/12` etc. from the hull's AABB, requiring
  one constant scale inside a body. The best candidate (int16, triple 4-5-6) is **33 % off**
  (spread 1.327), which is the same order as the AABB-vs-hull shape error — the oracle cannot
  certify a reading and no documented layout was obtainable, so the tensor stays UNKNOWN.

## 2. The partner, with an oracle and two controls (derived)

**Oracle.** Both bodies of a constraint must touch its pivot, so `|signed distance from the pivot to
the body's convex hull|` must be small. Signed distance = `max over planes of (n·p + d)` with
`jc3hkx`'s inward normals: ≤ 0 inside, ≈ 0 on the surface. 188 of the 494 cinfos are in files where
both body 0 and the cinfo body have a decoded polytope hull.

| tolerance | pair (body 0, cinfo body) | same pivot, shuffled partner | n |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.02 m | 0.074 | 0.000 | 188 |
| 0.05 m | **0.351** | **0.005** | 188 |
| 0.10 m | 0.543 | 0.016 | 188 |
| 0.20 m | 0.585 | 0.016 | 188 |

Median |distance| to the pivot: body 0 **0.0597 m**, the cinfo body **0.0139 m**.

**Control 2 — is body 0 special or just the biggest hull?** Same measurement for fixed ids:

| body | within 5 cm | measured | rate |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 91 | 188 | **0.484** |
| 1 | 13 | 181 | 0.072 |
| 2 | 12 | 188 | 0.064 |
| 3 | 15 | 154 | 0.097 |

So the cinfo's `+0x08` word is a part body and the partner is the chassis: 5× the next fixed body, 66× a
shuffled partner. Not called `decoded` because (a) only 48 % of pivots are within 5 cm of the chassis
surface — the rest lie inside it, which the oracle cannot separate from "not on body 0", and (b) a
constant 0 in every shipped record is equally consistent with a flag.

## 3. The two frames are one frame (decoded)

Each `hkpConstraintData` holds two 3×4 frames, A and B. Donor: **11/11** bit-identical translations,
rotations within **0.05°**, all deltas proper rotations (det 1). Positions per constraint A/B: 2, or 3
for the two ragdolls. Consequences:

- the constraintData names the joint's position and axis but **not** which body is on which side;
- G3-10c's idea of recovering the partner from "pivot frames consistent with body positions" cannot
  work, because both frames are the same point and the same orientation. §2's hull test replaces it.

Donor constraints (11): hinges on bodies 1, 3, 4, 6, 16, 19, 20, 26, 35 and ragdolls on 7, 18. Every
pivot is within 5 cm of its own body's hull except the two ragdolls on 7 and 18 (7.4 cm and 2.9 cm),
and within 5 cm of body 0 except the ragdoll at (0.031, 0.660, −2.389), which is 1.19 m from the
chassis hull — a rear-mounted joint outside the chassis envelope. `-` in the "|d| to body b" column:
that body's shape is not a convex polytope (bodies 16 and 26), so no hull planes exist to measure.

## 4. The 24 packed bytes: six readings, all scored, none certified (UNKNOWN)

Mass link reproduced: `polytope + 0x20` → `hkRefCountedProperties + 0x10` → `hknpShapeMassProperties`
for **2011** hulls in 46 files. Mass is the IEEE f32 at `+0x28`. The candidate readings, each with its
best triple and the median per-body spread of `I_axis / value_axis`:

| reading | best triple | median spread | n | decodes? |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| 6 × IEEE f32 | 0-1-2 | 1.25e22 | 194 | no |
| 12 × int16 raw | 4-5-6 | **1.327** | 1955 | no |
| 12 × int16 / 16384 | 4-5-6 | 1.327 | 1955 | no |
| 12 × int16 / 32767 | 4-5-6 | 1.327 | 1955 | no |
| 12 × u16 raw | 4-5-6 | 1.327 | 1955 | no |
| 12 × IEEE half | 3-4-5 | 6,116 | 1890 | no |

Bar: 15 % per-axis agreement (spread ≤ 1.15). Nothing reaches it. Two honest observations:

- **The best candidate is not noise.** Triple 4-5-6 follows the *direction* of the box-inertia tensor
  at mean cos **0.966** (others 0.78–0.86), and its per-axis implied scale agrees within ~33 % inside
  a body — i.e. the three components do scale together, as one packed vector would.
- **The oracle cannot certify it.** The hull is not a box, so `I` from its AABB is off by ~30 % per
  axis. A 33 % residual is therefore inside the oracle's own error: a true packed inertia and a
  wrong-but-correlated triple are indistinguishable here.

Slot 7 is the one strongly monotone slot (ρ(mass) **0.87**, ρ(Ixx/Iyy/Izz) 0.92/0.94/0.89), which is
what a shared scale/exponent inside the block looks like — but its position, base and exponent are
not identified, so it is recorded as a hint, not used.

## 6. UNKNOWNs, and exactly what blocks each

1. **The inertia tensor.** Blocked by: no public Havok 2014 header or community reader for
   `hknpShapeMassProperties` could be obtained this session (web search returned no organic results;
   grep.app rate-limited; PyNifly's docs name the class but not its fields), and the AABB oracle is
   ~30 % sharp where the best candidate is 33 % off. Test that would settle it: integrate the true
   inertia tensor of each hull from its vertices/faces (needs face topology, which
   `jc3hkx.decode_polytope` does not yet expose) and require one of the 12 slots to reproduce it
   within 5 % — or any shipped `hknpShapeMassProperties` whose authoring pipeline is known.
2. **Whether `+0x04 = 0` is body 0 or a flag.** Blocked by: it is 0 in all 494 shipped records, so
   the bytes cannot separate the readings. What would settle it: one shipped physicsc with a non-zero
   word there, or a per-body constraint list (the body entry has none: `+0x00` is the shape, `+0x20`
   the property node, and no other slot resolves to an array of constraint indices).
3. **The `+0x01` flag at bodies 11/15/27** (G3-10c U-3): unchanged, no decoded meaning.
4. **The remaining body-entry words:** unchanged from G3-10b's measured domains.
