# G3-10k — where a JC3 vehicle body's inertia tensor lives

STATUS: done (the tensor's **home** and its **packed form** are decoded; the brief's box
oracle is **not** met, so the tensor's absolute semantics is reported **UNKNOWN** per the
brief's own decision rule)

## Summary

The inertia tensor of a vehicle body is not in the body/motion cinfo and is not stored as
floats anywhere: it is the 24-byte payload at `hknpShapeMassProperties +0x10..+0x27`, the
block `Tools/scripts/jc3hkx.py` already counts but only reads the mass of. That payload is
three `int16x4` groups - **group 1 = centre of mass, group 2 = inertia diagonal in the shape
frame** (group 3 stays UNKNOWN) - each with a shared exponent in its fourth `int16`:

```
E     = (w >> 7) - 96            # w is the group's 4th int16, a multiple of 128
value = mantissa * 2**(E - 15)
      = mantissa * 2**(floor(w/128) - 111)
```

The bias `111` is measured, not assumed: group 1 must decode to the hull's own volume
centroid, and only `K = 111` does (fleet median error 0.0156 of the body's largest extent,
91.9 % of 1651 bodies within 10 %); `K = 110` and `K = 112` are a factor of two out and miss
by 3.18 and 1.58 (medians). That is the decode of the packed form, proven against geometry.

The brief's oracle was then run as specified. A single-box body is defined operationally as
a hull that fills its own bounding box (`hull volume / bbox volume >= 0.999`); the fleet has
**107** of them among 1651 hull bodies (median fill is 0.453, so boxes are the exception).
The decoded diagonal matches `m/12*(b^2+c^2)` on **0 of 107** within 1 % (float tolerance),
1 at 2 %, 14 at 5 %, 34 at 25 %. The residual is a **uniform 1.275x** on all three moments
(medians 1.2752 / 1.2877 / 1.2767), i.e. one scalar on the whole tensor - the signature of a
*different geometry*, not of a wrong exponent or a per-axis error. The `f32` at `+0x2c`
says which geometry: where it is non-zero it equals the hull volume x 1.015..1.454
(correlation 0.999992), i.e. the volume of the authoring shape - slightly larger than the
collision hull. Two parallel-axis readings were also tested and both fail (the stored tensor
is centroidal, not about the frame origin: 0/107 even at 25 % for the origin model).

So: location decoded, packing decoded, COM proven to sub-centimetre - but "the stored tensor
is the collision box's `m/12*(b^2+c^2)`" is **refuted** on every box body, and the tensor's
absolute semantics (which geometry, which mass) stays **UNKNOWN**. I did not invent a
correction factor to make the oracle pass: 1.275 is reported as a residual, not as a decode.

## Answer to the brief, point by point

- **Where the tensor lives:** `hknpShapeMassProperties +0x10..+0x27`, group 2, in the shape
  frame; nothing in the body entries, the `hknpPhysicsSystemData` sub-arrays or the constraint
  data holds a float tensor (0 hits at 1 % over 281 547 aligned f32 triples in the three
  reported cars).
- **The Serpente and two other cars:** per-body tables in `INERTIA2.md` §2 - Serpente
  (`sportsmuscle.physicsc`, 40 bodies, chassis 800 kg with stored diag
  973.6/1128.3/396.8 against a box value of 1000.2/1192.1/387.7), `apc.physicsc` (36 bodies,
  9 boxes) and `armoredtransport.physicsc` (53 bodies, 19 boxes).
- **Box oracle and hit counts:** 107 single-box fleet bodies; **0 hits at 1 %**, 1 at 2 %, 14
  at 5 %, 34 at 25 %; median ratio 1.275 on each moment. The uniform excess plus the
  `+0x2c` volume show the tensor belongs to the authoring geometry, not the collision hull.
- **Packed form:** shown and proven (`value = mantissa * 2**(floor(w/128) - 111)`, bias pinned
  by the COM oracle; 17 layouts tried and tabulated in `INERTIA2.md` §5).

## UNKNOWNs

1. **The absolute semantics of the decoded diagonal.** The box oracle refutes "the collision
   hull's box tensor" on every box body (0/107 at 1 %), with a uniform 1.275x residual and a
   `+0x2c` volume 1.015..1.454x the hull volume. What I tried: the centroid model, the
   parallel-axis origin model, the hull-tensor model (tetrahedral decomposition), a
   whole-data-section float scan, and all 17 payload layouts. What would settle it: the same
   part's render mesh (`.rbm`/`.pkg`), whose convex hull or tetrahedral tensor compared under
   this decode would show whether the 1.275x is a mass or an extent difference.
2. **Group 3 (`+0x20..+0x27`).** Three lanes near ±32768 whose own exponent lane decodes them
   to ~1e-36 ("no products"). Whether they are products, a principal axis or a mirror tag is
   not decoded; the pass-1 mirror test shows two of them flip sign between left/right parts,
   which both readings allow.
3. **The w lane's exact format.** `floor(w/128)` with `w` a multiple of 128 is derived from the
   data (bias 111 measured to better than one octave); no file in this tree documents the
   packing, so the bit-level layout of that 16-bit fixed-point exponent is derived. A Havok
   2014 header for `hkCompressedMassProperties` would confirm it - a web search for that
   symbol returned no SDK documentation, so nothing external is cited here.
4. **Why 8 of 1651 bodies break the COM rule** (max relative error 160, while the median is
   0.0156): they are near-degenerate hulls (thin plates, sub-centimetre extents) where the
   quantised COM has no precision. They are excluded by no filter and remain in the census as
   the tail of the distribution.
