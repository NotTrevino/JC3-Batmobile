# G3-10g - the constraint's second body: three places searched, none names it

STATUS: done

The partner stays **UNKNOWN**, and that is the finding, not a failure: every stored reading is
excluded by the bytes and every inferred reading is a coin toss. Five candidates were scored over
all 50 fleet files (494 constraint records, 3556 bodies); no candidate names the partner for
every record, so the report gives each one's score and says what would settle it.

| id | candidate | where | fits | holds? |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| C1 | cinfo `+0x04 == 0` -> partner is body 0 | inside the 24-byte cinfo | 113/228 (49.6%) | no |
| C2 | a parallel array beside the cinfo list, one body id per constraint | `hknpPhysicsSystemData` / data section | 0/494 (the array does not exist) | no |
| C3 | the constraintData's two pivot frames are the two bodies' local frames | constraintData | 0/484 (one point, not two) | no |
| C4 | nearest hull to the pivot, other than the recorded body | geometry only | 254/494 (51.4%), ambiguous in 98 | no |
| C5 | the body of the recorded body's **parent part** in the .epe | vehicle .epe part tree | 99/194 (51.0%) vs body 0's 62/131 (47.3%) | no |

The one positive structural result: **the constraints pair the .epe part tree**. In all 45 fleet
files that have a parts row, every constrained body is a part that has a parent - 439 of 439
cases - and the body->parent maps of different entity variants that share one `.physicsc` agree
exactly (0 disagreements). So the card's third suggestion is the right *shape* of answer (the tree
does pair the parts a constraint joins), but the parent's body is not always where the hinge sits:
in the Serpente the front bumper's parent is the engine and the pivot is 0.77 m from the engine's
hull while it is 0.035 m from body 0's. That is why C5 is a 51% fit and not a rule.

Two of G3-10d's readings are also corrected here, both from measuring rather than re-asserting:

- the "two frames" are one point in **482 of 484** fleet records (not just in the donor), so they
  cannot name two bodies at all - H2 fails structurally, not statistically;
- the counterexamples reproduce: 37 records have a body other than 0 **and** other than the
  recorded one within 5 cm while body 0 is over 0.20 m away, 68 when the recorded body is allowed
  to be that near body (the review's 65 counted over its own 188-record subset; the subsets differ,
  the phenomenon does not).

## UNKNOWN

- **The true second body of a constraint.** Tried: both id words of the cinfo (one is always 0,
  the other is the recorded body - 494/494), a size-matched parallel array anywhere in the data
  section (absent in all 50 files), both pivot frames in world and body-local space (the same
  point in 482/484, and the two readings are indistinguishable on the records that can tell them
  apart), the .epe parent link (right joints, wrong geometry), and the nearest-hull fit (ambiguous
  in 98 records). Where it would be found: a body-side constraint array (the 96-byte body entry
  resolves to a shape and a property node and nothing that indexes the cinfo list) or a Havok 2014
  `hknpConstraintCinfo` layout, which this tree does not contain.
- **Whether the constant 0 at `+0x04` is a body id or a flag.** It is 0 in all 494 shipped
  records, so the bytes cannot separate the readings.
- **Why body 0 fits so well.** 113 of 228 records put the pivot within 5 cm of the chassis hull -
  what a hinge to the chassis looks like, and also what a large central hull looks like for any
  joint near the mid-line. The oracle cannot separate those two.
- **Runtime meaning.** No game was launched or attached to; every number is a file fact.
