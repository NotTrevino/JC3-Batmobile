Element byte size (derived):
`4 + 12·nV + 4 + 16·nP + 4 + 4·nI + 4 + 12·nE + 4 + 12·nX + 4 + 16·nY + 4 + 4·nZ + 24 + 14`.

**Hypothesis verdict.** Confirmed for the six arrays and the box; **rejected as stated** on two
points: (a) there is a seventh counted array (`nZ`, 4-byte records) between the planes and the
box; (b) the box is 24 bytes (Min/Max) with no validity byte inside it — the 14 bytes behind it
are a constant trailer starting with `int32 1`. This also explains G3-01b's stop: it read a
"Z count" where the box begins, and the first box float is not a count.

## Limitations

- The `--check` plane oracle is an invariant on the *layout* (at most one near-miss triangle per
  element, and every miss parallel to a stored plane), not a claim that the authored hull is
  geometrically perfect. The three near misses are reported with exact numbers rather than
  rounded to "pass".
- Element 0 was decoded independently by G3-01b; the cross-check asserts the two records agree,
  which is the regression guard if either tool changes.
- Nothing here assigns a physical role to any element (which part of the car a hull is). That is
  G3-01's/G3-10's question; this record only decodes the geometry with its oracles.
