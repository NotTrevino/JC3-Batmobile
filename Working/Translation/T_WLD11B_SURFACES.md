# T-WLD11b - the hood and roof surfaces, and candidate stunt-area targets

Generated by `Tools/scripts/t_wld11b.py` from the donor entity, the donor geom
sidecar (lod1 car), the Batmobile body mesh and the B-04 part table. `--check`
re-derives every number and this file byte-for-byte, re-runs the oracles and fails if
a verdict moved.

## Verdict

Two candidates are computed for every area: **R-1** (strict proportional blanket)
and **R-2** (the same row, both columns on one standing plate). The oracles judge
R-2, because R-1 is the evidence for why R-2 exists:

| area | R-1 vertices on a standing point | R-2 verdict | O-1 vertices in band | O-2 interiors | O-3 shape |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `front_area` | 2/6 | candidate fails: O-2 | 6/6 | **fail** | pass |
| `front_signature_area` | 2/6 | candidate fails: O-2 | 6/6 | **fail** | pass |
| `main_area` | 6/10 | candidate fails: O-1, O-2 | 4/10 | **fail** | pass |

All 3 areas bind existing `DeformPoints` indices (22/22 of the bound indices resolve, range 0..134), so **no target needs an index that does not exist**. What the geometry cannot do with the existing vertex counts is

## 4. The rules, verbatim

**R-1 - proportional blanket, clamped to the target patch**

Inputs (all decoded):
- the area's decoded vertices3D_local
- the donor host band: standing donor tris in the area's z span
- the Batmobile standing body tris in the same normalized length band
- the decoded per-vertex clearance to the donor skin

Steps:
1. row z = the vertex's z fraction of the host band, applied to the target patch's own standing z extent
2. column x = the vertex's x fraction of the host band, applied to the target patch's standing x extent within +-0.35 m of that row
3. y = ray-cast top at (x, z) + decoded clearance along the hit face's up normal
4. clamp z toward the patch centre in 0.02 m steps when that (x, z) has no standing top

**R-2 - same row z as R-1, but both columns of a row are placed on ONE standing plate: the x-run of standing top surface with the largest overlap with R-1's span**

- why: R-1's span crosses the Batmobile's troughs; R-2 keeps the donor's column fractions but references them to a single plate, so a row cannot straddle two plates and no vertex lands in a trough
- COMPROMISE: the row's absolute width becomes the plate's width rather than the donor's fraction of the whole band; O-3 measures the error

The clamp is the only discretionary step, and it is deterministic: it moves z toward
the patch centre in 0.02 m steps, at fixed x, until the top surface is standing. It
fires only where the Batmobile has no standing top at the mapped (x, z); the distance
moved is in the `clamp` column above, so the distortion is measured, not hidden.

## 5. The oracles

### O-1 - every target vertex is a legal standing point

A target vertex passes when (a) the ray found a top surface, (b) its exact
point-triangle distance to `body_lod1.rbm` is inside the donor band 0.0389 - 0.1578 m, (c) its nearest face is standing (up-cos >= 0.7071), and (d) it is outside the shell.

| area | pass | of |
|---|---:|---:|
| `front_area` | 6 | 6 |
| `front_signature_area` | 6 | 6 |
| `main_area` | 4 | 10 |

### O-2 - the surface between the vertices does not clip or float away

Every target triangle is sampled on a 4x4 barycentric grid (4x4); each sample must be outside the shell and no further
than **0.075 m** from it - the donor's own interior looseness,
so the Batmobile is held to the standard the donor itself meets.

| area | samples | max interior gap (m) | inside | failing triangles |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `front_area` | 40 | 0.203 | 6 | 4 |
| `front_signature_area` | 40 | 0.203 | 6 | 4 |
| `main_area` | 80 | 0.2864 | 35 | 8 |

### O-3 - the area keeps its shape and its indices

Every bound index exists; the target's footprint fractions of its own patch stay
within **0.1** of the donor's fractions of its host band.

| area | indices in range | shape error | pass |
|---|---|---:|:-:|
| `front_area` | True | 0.0 | yes |
| `front_signature_area` | True | 0.0 | yes |
| `main_area` | True | 0.0 | yes |

## 6. What the candidates measure (the amendment, not a 'JC3 cannot')

| area | R-1 strict: vertices on a standing point | R-2 O-1 | R-2 interior samples inside the shell | R-2 max interior gap / donor limit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:| 
| `front_area` | 2/6 | 6/6 | 6/40 | 0.203 / 0.075 |
| `front_signature_area` | 2/6 | 6/6 | 6/40 | 0.203 / 0.075 |
| `main_area` | 6/10 | 4/10 | 35/80 | 0.2864 / 0.075 |

The Batmobile's top is a central spine between troughs, with separate fender plates
outboard. Two things follow from the numbers above, and neither is a 'JC3 cannot':

- A **two-column ribbon** can put every one of its vertices on a standing point (the
  front areas do), but the triangles between those points cross a trough, so part of
  the surface is *inside* the shell: O-2 counts those samples. The area would be
  standable at its corners and wrong between them.
- Where a row's proportional span is wider than any single plate, R-2 has to choose
  one plate per row, and rows on different plates meet in triangles that cannot
  follow the body. That is what the interior counts measure for `main_area`.

Options in order of lore fidelity, for the operator to choose:

1. **Add columns** - a conformal drape needs more vertices per row, i.e.
   `DeformPoints` indices above 134, and those **do not exist
   today**: adding them is a change to the deform module (a build step, not this
   analysis). The `StuntAreas` module itself stores room for them - its vertex
   array is 48 floats = 16 vertices per area, and its largest triangle array
   is 42 indices = 14 triangles,
   against the 10 vertices and 8 triangles the largest donor
   area uses. So a 3-column ribbon (12 vertices) fits the decoded arrays; whether
   the triangle array grows past that is UNKNOWN (§7).
2. **Narrow the blanket to one plate** - keep every index and triangle, follow the
   central spine or one plate, and accept less coverage than the donor's fraction.
   The `coverage` column above is exactly what is given up, and O-3 measures it as
   the difference between the target row's share of the whole standable row and the
   donor's share of its host band.
3. **Widen to the fender plates** - the `nearest standing plateau` and `plate_x_m`
   columns show where that lands; it keeps the donor's width but the blanket then
   floats over the troughs, which is the interior gap O-2 reports.

Whichever is chosen, it is a COMPROMISE from the donor's proportional relation and
must be approved, not silently shipped.

## 7. UNKNOWNs

- **Which field the runtime trusts** - the stored `vertices3D_local` or a recomputation
  from `DeformPoints` (T-WLD11's O-2). Nothing in the entity, the sidecar or the
  build's modules states it; the candidate above is a target, not a working edit.
- **Whether the runtime requires the area to be planar or convex.** The donor's areas
  are neither exactly planar (residual 0.2545 m)
  nor convex in the vertical sense, so nothing constrains the Batmobile target beyond
  what the donor itself does.
- **Whether the Batmobile's add-on parts** (turret, missile pods, aero flaps) sit above
  the hood/roof band. `body_lod1.rbm` is the body only; their placement lives in the
  part transforms, which this task did not decode.
- **How many triangles an area may carry.** The entity stores
  `stunt_areas[*].triangle_indices` up to index 41 (14 triangles) because that is the largest donor area; the vertex array is exactly 48 floats (3 x 16), so 16 vertices are representable.
  Whether the triangle array can be grown past that is not stated in the entity. What
  was tried: the raw `StuntAreas` row paths (counted in §1); settling it needs the
  `.vmodc` module decoded.
