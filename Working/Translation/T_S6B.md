# T-S6B - JC3 suspension parameters vs BmAK's suspension fields

Generated by `Tools/scripts/t_s6b_suspension.py`. `--check` re-reads the grab, the BmSDK
layout and this oracle and rebuilds the file byte for byte. Read-only: no install write, no
game launch.

## Answer

**The carrier exists and the stiffness bridge exists in form; its numbers are UNKNOWN.**

JC3 has a dedicated `Suspension` module (49 rows on the donor), not a
slot inside the handling module. It carries a spring rate, two damper rates (bump and rebound,
split into separate members), a spring length with min length and a length offset, a per-axle
anti-roll strength, and a hardpoint offset along the spring axis - the closest member to BmAK's
`SuspensionBias`. What it does **not** carry is a separate droop figure: only total travel.

On the stiffness side the unit question is settled by the fleet itself (section 3): JC3
`strength` is **mass-normalised** (s^-2), so the bridge to BmAK's absolute spring rate is
`k = strength * mass`. That bridge is therefore *formally* available for every vehicle, and it
needs one number this project does not have: BmAK's stiffness value in a known unit.

## 3. The oracle: is a unit bridge needed, and which reading survives?

A static spring must compress less than its own length. For a four-wheel car each spring
carries `m*g/4`, so the sag is `m*g/(4*k)`. Read `strength` as mass-normalised and `m`
cancels; read it as absolute N/m or kN/m and the sag grows with the vehicle's mass.

Tested on **166** vehicles that carry a `Suspension` module and a `RigidBody.mass`
(mass 50-550000 kg, median 2000, 65 above 3,000 kg):

| reading of `strength` | sag = | fits inside `length` | violates |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| mass-normalised (s^-2) | g/(4*strength) | 164/166 | 2 |
| absolute N/m | m*g/(4*strength) | 0/166 | 166 |
| absolute kN/m | m*g/(4*strength*1000) | 92/166 | 74 |

The decisive vehicle is the heaviest one, `v0005_car_autostraad_minetruck`:

- decoded: mass 550000 kg, `front.strength` 30, `front.length` 0.180000007 m;
- mass-normalised reading: sag 0.0818 m - inside the 0.180000007 m spring;
- N/m reading: sag 44962.5 m - 249792x
  the spring's own length, i.e. the car would sit on its bump stops before the wheel touched
- kN/m reading: sag 44.9625 m - also longer than the spring.

The mass-normalised reading misses on 2 rows, and all of them are the
same car (a civilian and its debug twin) overshooting a very short spring by a hair:

| vehicle | mass (kg) | strength | length (m) | sag (m) | sag/length |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `v0510_car_solar_modernroadster` (v0510_car_solar_modernroadster_civilian_01.ee) | 1000 | 20 | 0.12 | 0.1226 | 1.0217 |
| `v0510_car_solar_modernroadster` (v0510_car_solar_modernroadster_debug.ee) | 1000 | 20 | 0.12 | 0.1226 | 1.0217 |

The worst of them is 1.0217x the spring length - a 2% overshoot that
says the car rides within a few millimetres of its bump stop, not that the unit is wrong.

So `strength` cannot be an absolute rate: the N/m reading is falsified on **every** vehicle, the
kN/m reading fails on the heavy half, and the mass-normalised reading fits the whole fleet to
within 1.0217x. This is the oracle the brief asks for - it would fail if
`strength` were N/m, and it does.

Consistency of the other members under the same normalisation (derived): damping ratio
`zeta = compression/(2*sqrt(strength))` spans 0.079..0.775
(median 0.198) and the natural frequency `sqrt(strength)/(2*pi)` spans
0.318..1.424 Hz (median 0.872) - road-car
plausible ranges for all 166 vehicles, with no mass term needed. Label: derived.

## 5. What T-S6 got right, and what it did not

- **Right**: there is no droop member. JC3 has `length`, `min_length` and
  `max_length_offset`; on the donor `min_length` = 0 and `max_length_offset` = 0, so the spring
  has total travel and no encoded down-travel split, while BmAK keeps `BMFrontWheelMaxDroop`
  and `BMRearWheelMaxDroop` as separate fields.
- **Right**: there is no decoded numeric bridge for stiffness - BmAK's stiffness value and
  unit are not in any project file.
- **Wrong** in the implied conclusion that the parameters are absent: the `Suspension` module
  carries the spring rate, both damper rates, the anti-roll strength and a rest offset along the
  spring, and the mass-normalised unit of `strength` is now decoded by the oracle above.
- **Bias**: `hardpoint_offset_along_spring` is the member that plays BmAK's bias role
  (a static offset along the spring axis, non-zero on the donor). Whether BmAK's bias means the
  same thing, and at what sign and scale, is **UNKNOWN**.
