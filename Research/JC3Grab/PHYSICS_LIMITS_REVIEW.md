# Review of G3-10e: are the JC3 joint limits really at +0x0FC/+0x100?

Generated by `Tools/scripts/jc3_physics_limits2.py`. `decoded` = read at a stated offset; `derived` = a rule over decoded inputs; `UNKNOWN` = nothing pins it.

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED.** G3-10e's offsets are right, and the reason they are right is now decoded rather than plausible: the game's own binary names the atom at `+0x0F8` `TYPE_ANG_LIMIT`, and the Havok SDK header puts `m_minAngle` at atom+4 and `m_maxAngle` at atom+8 in a 16-byte atom. So in the hinge object the pair sits at `+0x0FC`/`+0x100`, the `+0x104` float is `m_angularLimitsTauFactor`, and the atom ends at `+0x108`. A door/hood geometry test agrees with the sign and the units.

What this review adds to G3-10e, and what it leaves alone:

| claim in G3-10e | this review |
|---|---|
| hinge limit atom is type code 14 at `+0x0F8` | **confirmed by name**: the exe's `AtomType` enum says 14 = `TYPE_ANG_LIMIT` |
| atom type names are UNKNOWN | **closed**: every code in the fleet now has a name |
| min at `+0x0FC`, max at `+0x100`, factor at `+0x104` | **confirmed by the header's field order**, and by `isEnabled`/`limitAxis`/tau bytes |
| "not a neighbouring motor atom" | **confirmed two ways**: the motor atom ends at `+0x0E8`, and the fleet's plain hinges have no type-14 atom at all |
| ragdoll: three limit atoms, which is which UNKNOWN | **half closed**: 15 = `TYPE_TWIST_LIMIT`, 16 twice = `TYPE_CONE_LIMIT`; which cone is which axis stays UNKNOWN |
| limits in radians, min <= max | **confirmed**, plus a physical anchor (a door at 80 deg, a hood at 70 deg, a trunk at 90 deg) |

Fleet read for this pass: **419 hinges** and **71 ragdolls** over 51 `.physicsc` files, plus the **4** plain `hkpHingeConstraintData` the earlier pass never looked at (extents [304] / [224] / [416]).

## 1. The atom type enum, from the game's own binary

`D:/Games/Just Cause 3/JustCause3.exe` (51083264 bytes) carries Havok's reflection enum for `hkpConstraintAtom::AtomType` as a run of `TYPE_*` strings at **0x2552848**. In file order the run is *descending* by value; read bottom-up and anchored at `TYPE_BRIDGE = 1` it gives:

**Oracle:** values 1..20 must equal the order in the public header (`https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nitaigao/engine-showcase/master/etc/vendor/havok/Source/Physics/ConstraintSolver/Constraint/Atom/hkpConstraintAtom.h`, quoted in the JSON) - **pass**. The published Havok 2014.1 enum continues `TYPE_PULLEY, TYPE_RACK_AND_PINION, TYPE_COG_WHEEL, TYPE_SETUP_STABILIZATION, ...`, which is exactly what the exe lists, so the JC3 numbering is the modern one, not the 2009 one.

This matters for one atom in particular: **23**. The 2009 header has 23 = a soft-contact *modifier*, but the JC3 exe has 23 = `TYPE_SETUP_STABILIZATION`, and the 16-byte atom at `+0x0B0` of both classes is a plain setup-stabilization atom. Using the old header for the names would have labelled it wrongly - which is why the exe run, not the header, is the authority for values above 20.

The hinge's atom sequence - `SET_LOCAL_TRANSFORMS, SETUP_STABILIZATION, ANG_MOTOR, ANG_FRICTION, ANG_LIMIT, 2D_ANG, BALL_SOCKET` - is exactly the set a limited hinge needs and nothing else, and the ragdoll's - `..., RAGDOLL_MOTOR, ANG_FRICTION, TWIST_LIMIT, CONE_LIMIT, CONE_LIMIT, BALL_SOCKET` - is the ragdoll set. Every atom that can be sized matches the header: `ANG_LIMIT` 16, `ANG_FRICTION` 16, `TWIST_LIMIT` 32, `CONE_LIMIT` 32, `RAGDOLL_MOTOR` 96, `SET_LOCAL_TRANSFORMS` 144. The last atom of each class is measured to the object end, so its row is not a size test.

**Two honest divergences from the 2009 header** (the game is 2014.1): the ANG_MOTOR atom is **40 B** in the file and 32 B in the 2009 header, and `TYPE_SETUP_STABILIZATION` does not exist in 2009 at all (16 B here). Neither touches the limit atoms, and both are reported rather than glossed; the `no_unexpected_size_mismatch` oracle fails if any *other* atom disagrees.

## 3. The limit fields, at their header offsets

`hkpAngLimitConstraintAtom` in the header is, in order: `m_type` (u16), `m_isEnabled` (u8), `m_limitAxis` (u8), `m_minAngle` (f32, "radians, may be negative"), `m_maxAngle` (f32), `m_angularLimitsTauFactor` (f32, default 1.0, clamped to [0,1]) = 16 B. Placed at `+0x0F8`:

| field | offset | in the donor (front-left door, 0x4DA80) | fleet |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `m_type` | +0x0F8 | 14 (`TYPE_ANG_LIMIT`) | 419/419 |
| `m_isEnabled` | +0x0FA | 1 | 1 in 419/419 |
| `m_limitAxis` | +0x0FB | 0 | 0 in 419/419 |
| **`m_minAngle`** | **+0x0FC** | **0 deg** | 192/419 negative, all within +/-pi |
| **`m_maxAngle`** | **+0x100** | **80 deg** | 181/419 above 1 rad |
| `m_angularLimitsTauFactor` | +0x104 | 1.0 | [1.0] in 419/419 |

Three things that could have broken the claim, and did not:

1. **Wrong field order (min/max swapped).** `min <= max` holds in 419/419 hinges. A swap would fail roughly half of them.
2. **`+0x0FC` being the tau factor.** Excluded by the data: 192 hinges have a **negative** min and 181 have a max **above 1 rad (57.3 deg)**, while the header clamps the tau factor to [0,1]. The byte that is the tau (the one that does hold exactly 1.0) is `+0x104`.
3. **The pair belonging to the motor atom.** The `ANG_MOTOR` atom starts at `+0x0C0` and runs 40 B, so it ends at `+0x0E8`; `ANG_FRICTION` occupies `+0x0E8..+0x0F7`; the limit atom is `+0x0F8..+0x107`. `+0x0FC` is 4 B into the limit atom and 20 B past the motor's end. Independent confirmation: the fleet's **plain** hinges have no limit atom and no motor atom at all:

| file | offset | extent | atom codes at the hinge's starts | limit atom? |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| `mediumoldfishing.physicsc` | 0x817B0 | 224 | 2, 23, 12 | no |
| `mediumoldfishing.physicsc` | 0x81890 | 224 | 2, 23, 12 | no |
| `mediumoldfishing.physicsc` | 0x81970 | 224 | 2, 23, 12 | no |
| `transport.physicsc` | 0x25910 | 224 | 2, 23, 12 | no |

304 - 224 = 80 B is exactly the motor (40) + friction (16) + limit (16) + the frame split (8) that a *limited* hinge adds. So the atom that only exists in limited hinges is the limit atom, and the limits live inside it.

## 4. Units

- The header says `m_minAngle`/`m_maxAngle` are **radians** (quoted in the JSON).
- 419/419 hinges put both values inside +/-pi, which a degrees convention would break (the median max is 80, not 1.4).
- 405/419 sit on a 0.5-degree grid in degrees, i.e. the authored values are degrees converted to radians; the 14 exceptions are per-car hand-tuned angles (see the JSON).
- 64 distinct (min,max) pairs ship: (-180, 180), (-179, 179), (-90, 0), (-75.621, 0), (-75, 75), (-60, 0), (-50, 0), (-50, 25), (-50, 50), (-45, 0), (-45, 45), (-35, 0).

Read the front-left door: pivot at x = -1.018 (outboard, front edge of the door), centroid at x = -0.837. At the allowed +80 deg the centroid goes to x = -1.677 - **+0.840 m further from the centreline**, i.e. the door swings out; at -80 deg (outside the range) it goes to x = -0.296, -0.540 m, **into the chassis**. The front-right door is the mirror image: its axis is (0, +1, 0) instead of (0, -1, 0) and its range is the same 0..80, so both doors open outward. The hood (behaviour `Hood`) rises +0.843 m at 70 deg and would sink into the engine bay in the forbidden direction; the trunk rises +1.064 m at 90 deg.

That is the units-and-sign check in one: 70/80/90 **degrees** of a panel whose size is ~0.8 m produce 0.8 m of swing, which is what the game shows; reading the same floats as degrees (80 rad = 13 full turns) or with the sign flipped would drive every panel through the car. Every panel with a rule passes: **5/5**.

319 of 334 rows have min = 0 (closed at zero) and 5 have max = 0 (the one-sided mirrors and bumpers). The dominant pairing is *axis sign = side*: of the 209 rows whose hinge axis lies in the car's Y, **193** point the axis to the same side as the pivot (left doors `(0,-1,0)`, right doors `(0,+1,0)`) with a `0..80` range on both - which is what makes the same positive angle open both doors outward.

The other **16** rows are a real exception, not a rounding artefact: they are four assets where the axis is paired with the opposite side (a bus rear door `0..80`, a helicopter door `-1..1`, a helicopter door `-90..0`, a cargo-plane interior door `0..90`; listed in the JSON). Under the donor's convention those panels open with the opposite sign - either they open inward or their part frame is mirrored. That is **UNKNOWN** here (it needs each part's centroid, which this pass decoded only for the donor), and it does not touch the decode: the bytes are what they are, only their reading on those four assets is open.

## 6. The ragdoll's three limit atoms, now named

| atom | name | extent | min at | max at | tau at | min <= max | distinct pairs | tau |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---|
| +0x130 | `TYPE_TWIST_LIMIT` | 32 B | +0x138 | +0x13C | +0x140 | 71/71 | 15 | [0.8] |
| +0x150 | `TYPE_CONE_LIMIT` | 32 B | +0x158 | +0x15C | +0x160 | 71/71 | 17 | [0.8] |
| +0x170 | `TYPE_CONE_LIMIT` | 32 B | +0x178 | +0x17C | +0x180 | 71/71 | 7 | [0.8] |

The header's `hkpTwistLimitConstraintAtom` and `hkpConeLimitConstraintAtom` both put `m_minAngle` at atom+8, `m_maxAngle` at atom+12 and the tau factor at atom+16 - exactly where G3-10e read them - and both are 32 B, exactly the observed extent. So that half of G3-10e's UNKNOWN list closes: 15 is the **twist** limit, 16 is a **cone** limit, and the ragdoll ships **two** cone atoms (a 2014 addition; the 2009 header has one). Which cone bounds which axis, and what the -100 rad min selects (it reads exactly -100.0 in 71/71 ragdolls), stay UNKNOWN.

## 7. What this review did not change

G3-10e's numbers stand as written: the fleet counts, the donor table, the 34/66 audit of G3-10b's column, and every oracle it listed. This pass adds names, an atom-size proof, and a physical sign test; it found **no** case where a value G3-10e called a limit is not one.

## 9. UNKNOWNs

- **ANG_MOTOR is 40 B here vs 32 B in the 2009 header.** The extra 8 B are not identified; no header for 2014.1 is available. It does not touch the limit atoms.
- **`TYPE_SETUP_STABILIZATION`'s fields.** The atom is 16 B and constant per class, but 2009 has no such struct, so its layout is not pinned.
- **Which cone atom bounds which ragdoll axis** (the class has two). Nothing in the bytes orders them; a posed ragdoll or the 2014.1 ragdoll header would settle it.
- **The -100 rad sentinel's exact semantics.** It is the same value in every ragdoll and is far below any cone angle; "no lower limit" is `derived`, not decoded.
- **The sign convention on four assets** whose door axis is paired with the opposite side to their pivot (section 5). Their doors may open inward, or their part frames may be mirrored; closing it needs those parts' centroid and pivot in one space, which this pass did not decode.
- **The non-limit atoms' field meanings** (`SETUP_STABILIZATION`, the two halves of the frame atom, the friction torque) - unchanged from G3-10e.
