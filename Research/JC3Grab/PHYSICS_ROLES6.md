## 2. H2 - the constraintData's two frames (decoded: one point, not two)

Frames are located by the project's own `rot3x4` test (three unit rows, orthogonal, det +1)
sliding over the object; the translation follows the 3x4 rotation.

| measure | value |
|---|---:|
| records with two or more frames | 484 |
| of those, the two frames carry the **same** pivot | 482 |
| of those, the pivots differ | 2 |

The differing records, in full:

| file | cinfo | frame A | frame B |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `mediumoldfishing.physicsc` | 37 | `[0.0, 0.0, 0.0]` | `[0.0, -2.457, 8.521]` |
| `transport.physicsc` | 10 | `[-1.183678, -0.458021, 10.390186]` | `[-1.43603, -1.185458, 10.108314]` |

A pair of frames that is the same point cannot say which body is on which side, so H2 fails
structurally rather than statistically. The frame *convention* was still measured, because a
body-local frame would name the body it belongs to. On the 24 records whose recorded body
has a non-identity transform - the only records that can tell a world read from a body-local
read apart - the untouched (world) read puts the pivot within 5 cm on **17** and the
un-transformed (local) read on **18**; medians 0.0100 m and 0.0100 m. The two readings are
indistinguishable on this sample, so the frame convention is not settled by them either - and
a frame that cannot be placed in a body cannot name that body.

## 3. H3 - the .epe part tree (decoded: real link, wrong rule)

Structural check, over the 45 files that have parts: the constrained bodies are parts with a
parent in 439 of 439 cases; in 45 of those files **every** constrained body is a part with a
parent. That is why the parent link looked like the answer: it pairs exactly the joints the
constraints describe, in the Serpente and fleet-wide. It fails as the *body* rule because the
parent's own hull is often not where the hinge sits (front bumper -> engine: 0.77 m in the
Serpente, while body 0 is 0.035 m away), so a parent body is a plausible-side guess, not an
identified partner.
