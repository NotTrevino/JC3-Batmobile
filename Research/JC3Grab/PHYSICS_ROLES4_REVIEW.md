# PHYSICS_ROLES4_REVIEW - independent audit of G3-10d

Generated by `Tools/scripts/review_g310d.py --write`; `--check` re-derives every rate, distance, spread and source-text count from the install and fails on drift. No value is copied from `PHYSICS_ROLES4.json`; the shared decoder `jc3hkx.decode_polytope` is reused (the only hull reader in the tree), its output is re-measured here.

Labels: **decoded** = read from bytes here; **derived** = a rule applied to decoded inputs; **UNKNOWN** = not decodable from these bytes, blocker named.

## Verdict

**the oracle reproduces, the universal inference does not.** The oracle reproduces to the digit (188 measurable records, pair rate 0.351 at 5 cm, shuffled control 0.005, 2011 mass-linked hulls over 46 files), and the inertia UNKNOWN holds (no reading of 13 candidates reaches the bar; best spread 1.337, which becomes G3-10d's own 1.327 (1.327) once its 4-decimal rounding is re-applied). What does not survive is the universal inference: the recorded body carries the pivot **0.766** of the time against body 0's **0.484**, body 0 anchors a pivot on its own in only 25 of 188 records at 5 cm and in none past 10 cm, and **65** constraints have the 'A side' more than 0.20 m from the pivot while another body is within 5 cm - the worst 6.19 m away. Under a two-body reading with A = body 0 every one of those records is impossible, so the fleet-wide claim is false as stated; a chassis-side reading survives for part of the fleet only.

## The claim, verbatim

G3-10d, `PHYSICS_ROLES4.md` §2: *"So the cinfo's second body id is a part body and the partner is the chassis: body 0 is near the pivot 5x more often than the next fixed body, and the (0, cinfo-body) pair beats a shuffled partner by 66x at 5 cm."* Its own caveat: *"only 48% of the pivots are within 5 cm of the chassis hull surface, so the oracle has a floor"*, label `derived`, and UNKNOWN 2: whether `+0x04 = 0` is body 0 or a flag.

| word | values over 494 records |

All three are constants fleet-wide. Note what the reviewed tool tests: `cinfo_body_id_a_in_range` checks `0 <= a < n_bodies`, which for `a == 0` can never fail - the "0 in 494/494" sentence in its report is prose, not an oracle. This review asserts it directly.

## 2. The frame premise (decoded, and narrower than it looks)

A hull's planes come back in the shape's own space and the pivot is a stored translation, so the comparison is only valid for a body whose transform is identity. Measured: 447 of 3556 bodies are non-identity, spread over **48 of 50 files**; body 0 is identity in **every** file (yes). So every distance to body 0 - the A side of every record, and every counterexample in section 4 - is same-frame. Distances to the recorded body are same-frame for the 187 of 188 measurable records whose recorded body is identity. G3-10d relies on this premise without stating it; it is true for the side its claim depends on.

## 3. The partner test, including the comparison G3-10d never ran

G3-10d compares the pair against a *shuffled* body. The control that decides whether the pair reading is supported is the recorded body **alone**: if `+0x04` is a real partner id, then body 0 must be on the pivot as often as the recorded body is. It is not.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.02 m | 0.575 | 0.170 | 0.074 | 0.096 | 0.330 | 0.000 | 188 |
| 0.05 m | 0.766 | 0.484 | 0.351 | 0.133 | 0.101 | 0.005 | 188 |
| 0.10 m | 0.963 | 0.543 | 0.543 | 0.000 | 0.037 | 0.016 | 188 |
| 0.20 m | 0.984 | 0.585 | 0.585 | 0.000 | 0.016 | 0.016 | 188 |

Median |distance| to the pivot: recorded body **0.014 m**, body 0 **0.060 m**.

Two facts kill the pair reading, and both are visible in this table:

- **Body 0 anchors alone only at 5 cm.** `P(body 0 only)` is **25** records at 5 cm (0.133 of the set), **0** at 10 cm and **0** at 20 cm. So body 0 does contribute an independent anchor for about one record in eight, but only at the scale where its hull is already everywhere: past 10 cm it never covers a pivot the recorded body misses. A real second partner should be the near one exactly when the recorded body is far from the joint.
- **The recorded body is a better fit on its own** (0.766 vs 0.484 at 5 cm). The pair rate is not evidence that body 0 shares the joint; it is the rate at which a pivot that sits on the recorded body also happens to lie within 5 cm of the biggest hull in the file.

The reviewed card's control 2 (fixed ids 0..3) compares body 0 against parts 1, 2, 3, reproduced here:

| body | within 5 cm | measured | rate |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 91 | 188 | 0.484 |
| 1 | 13 | 181 | 0.072 |
| 2 | 12 | 188 | 0.064 |
| 3 | 15 | 154 | 0.097 |

That control is confounded by hull size - body 0 is the chassis, so any pivot inside the car is within a few centimetres of it. The recorded-body column above is the unconfounded comparison, and it favours the recorded body.

## 6. The frames (decoded)

11 of 11 donor constraints carry at least two 3x4 frames and **11** of those share one pivot, reproducing G3-10d's "11/11 one frame written twice"; every A->B delta is a proper rotation. So the frames cannot name which body is A - which is why the geometric test was the right idea; the error is in what the test was compared against. One artefact: the 2 ragdolls report a *third* frame whose pivot is exactly (0, 0, 0), because the detector scans 16-float runs and a zero-translation rotation exists in the same object. "Frame counts 2 or 3" is therefore a property of the detector, not of the constraint, and it is why G3-10d's own `pivots_equal` is False on those two records while `pivots_ab_equal` is True.

The extra layouts (6 x i32/u32, 24 x i8/u8, 3 x f64, first-12-byte variants) do not get closer, so G3-10d's UNKNOWN stands and is now harder to overturn. Its published 1.327 reproduces (`1.327`) only when the box inertia is rounded to 4 decimals the way its code does before the ratio; without that rounding the best i16 reading is `1.337`. The report's number therefore carries a small rounding artefact, not an error, and the conclusion (nothing decodes) is unaffected.

What G3-10d presents as six independent readings is really fewer. Scaling every int16 by one constant leaves max/min of the three ratios unchanged, so `raw`, `/16384` and `/32767` are the same test by construction; `u16` reinterprets the sign bit and is a genuinely different one. Measured, not asserted:

|---|---:|---|
| i16_16384 | 0.00e+00 | **yes** |
| i16_32767 | 0.00e+00 | **yes** |
| u16 | 2.19e+00 | no |

So the table's six rows are 11 distinct tests, and the one that matters (is there *any* constant-scale reading) is answered: no.

## 8. Script hygiene: what two concurrent edits left behind

`Tools/scripts/jc3_physics_roles4.py`, 783 lines. Counted from its own source:

| token | occurrences | what it is |
|---|---:|---|
| `NO_BODY` | 1 | constant 0x7FFFFFFF: defined, never compared |
| `quaternion` | 1 | body quaternion: collected, never read |
| `"position"` | 1 | body position: collected, never read |
| `word_0c` | 1 | cinfo +0x0C: collected, never read |
| `field_08` | 1 | body +0x08: collected, never read |
| `field_0c` | 1 | body +0x0C: collected, never read |
| `field_10` | 1 | body +0x10: collected, never read |
| `pivots_equal` | 3 | all-frames-equal: collected, only written to JSON |

- `NO_BODY = 0x7FFFFFFF` is defined and never compared, even though the card's §1 table claims that word is `0x7FFFFFFF` in every record - the claim is prose, not checked. Section 1 above checks it (it holds).
- `position` / `quaternion` are read for every body and then never used. That is the missing piece of the frame question: a version that transformed pivots into a body frame would have needed them. The shipped data has identity transforms, so their absence does not change the numbers - but the code cannot tell a reviewer that.
- `i16_16384` appears on 4 lines: once in the reading table and again hard-coded inside the direction test and the per-slot correlation, so those two analyses are not interpretation-independent although the report presents them as properties of "the block". Same for `/16384` vs `/32767`, which cannot differ (section 7).
- `pivots_equal` is computed, stored and never used by any oracle; the frame oracle iterates only records with two frames, so it would pass vacuously on a file where no constraint had two.
- No duplicated function definitions were found (each `def` is unique); the damage is dormant fields and a mis-stated comparison, not two copies of a function.

## 9. What survives, what does not

Survives (reproduced here, decoded): the cinfo record shape, `+0x08` as a valid body id, the constancy of `+0x04`/`+0x0C`/`+0x10`/`+0x14` (now actually asserted), body 0's identity transform in every file, the one-frame constraintData, the mass link, and the inertia UNKNOWN with its scoring.

Does not survive: **"the partner is body 0"** as a fleet-wide result. Its own data contradicts it in 65 of 188 measurable records (a pivot 6.19 m from the chassis while a part carries it), and the comparison that would support it - body 0 alone against the recorded body alone - goes the other way, 0.484 against 0.766. The strongest form the evidence still allows is narrower: body 0 is a plausible second side for the subset of records whose pivot lies inside or on the chassis (0.543 within 10 cm), and each constraint certainly names **one** body (`+0x08`) whose hull carries the pivot. The other side is not recorded in any field decoded so far, and `+0x04 = 0` is a constant whose meaning the shipping data cannot settle.

## 10. UNKNOWN

- **What `+0x04 = 0` is.** Tried: every cinfo in 50 files (all zero); geometry against the recorded body, body 0 alone, and the pair, with a shuffled control. The geometry refutes *"body 0 is the partner of every constraint"* but cannot say whether the word is a flag, an unset side, or a body id that every author left at 0. Where it would be found: a shipped physicsc with a non-zero word, or the engine's `hknpConstraintCinfo` reader, which is not in this tree.
- **The true partner of a constraint.** Not recorded in any field decoded so far: `+0x00` is the constraintData fixup, `+0x04` constant, `+0x08` one body, `+0x0C`/`+0x10`/`+0x14` constant, and the body entry carries no constraint list. Where it would be found: the body-side constraint array, if one exists, or a runtime reader.
- **The 24 packed inertia bytes.** Unchanged from G3-10d and now tested against 13 layouts; no reading reaches a constant per-body scale. Where it would be found: hull inertia integrated from face topology (`jc3hkx.decode_polytope` exposes planes, not faces), or a Havok 2014 header.
- **Runtime behaviour.** Every number here is a file fact; no game was launched or attached to.
