## 1. The candidate fields

A hinge object holds one frame atom (+0x020, type code 2) covering +0x020..+0x0B0: two `hkTransform`s, each three 16-byte rotation rows and a translation 16 bytes after them. The six rotation rows are the candidates:

| candidate | offset in the hinge object | what it is |
|---|---:|---|
| `A0` | +0x030 | frame A row 0 |
| `A1` | +0x040 | frame A row 1 |
| `A2` | +0x050 | frame A row 2 |
| `B0` | +0x070 | frame B row 0 |
| `B1` | +0x080 | frame B row 1 |
| `B2` | +0x090 | frame B row 2 |

Frame A's translation (the pivot) is at +0x060, frame B's at +0x0A0 (**decoded**: PHYSICS_ROLES2 §1, re-read here). The two frames are byte-identical in **410/419** hinges, and the two pivots coincide in **407/419** - so frame B repeats frame A and adds no independent candidate.

## 2. Oracle counts over all 419 hinges

| candidate | O1 unit | O2 perp. to pivot pair | O3 hull-extent, perpendicular | O3 hull-extent, parallel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `A0` | 419/419 | 0/12 | 151/402 | 33/402 |
| `A1` | 419/419 | 9/12 | 42/402 | 101/402 |
| `A2` | 419/419 | 1/12 | 68/402 | 54/402 |
| `B0` | 419/419 | 0/12 | 149/402 | 33/402 |
| `B1` | 419/419 | 9/12 | 42/402 | 101/402 |
| `B2` | 419/419 | 1/12 | 68/402 | 47/402 |

**O1 is not a discriminating oracle, and that is a result, not a gap:** all six rows are rows of one orthonormal frame, so every candidate is unit length in 419/419. It can falsify a layout but cannot choose between the six.

**O2 has no input in 407/419 hinges.** A Havok hinge places both atoms' pivots at the joint, so `pivotB - pivotA` is the zero vector and "the pivot-to-pivot axis" is undefined; the oracle applies to the **12** hinges where the two pivots differ (all of them in the project's own round-trip / shift / scale test copies, where the two frames were edited apart). Over that subset the best candidate is `A1`/`B1` at 9/12; `A0`/`B0` is 0/12. No candidate is perpendicular in all 12, and a 12-hinge subset cannot carry the fleet: O2 discriminates nothing that matters.

**O3 divides the two readings and passes neither.** As literally written ("agreement" = parallel) the best candidate is `A1`/`B1` at 101/402; read as perpendicularity (a door swings *around* an axis that crosses the panel-to-body direction) the best is `A0`/`B0` at 151/402. The two readings point at different columns and neither reaches a pass, so O3 does not select a layout. The host choice does not cause that: with the control rule (host = the first body entry, 245 hinges) the perpendicular counts are `A0` 112/245, `A1` 18/245, `A2` 42/245 - the same ordering.

Host resolution: the cinfo gives one body id (419/419 hinges resolve one), and the host body is found geometrically in 402/419 (largest non-compound body whose world AABB contains the pivot). The remaining hinges have a pivot no other body's AABB holds - mostly the project's shifted/scaled test copies - and are excluded from O3, not counted as passes.

Every candidate is axis-aligned most of the time: the hinge frames are authored in the car frame, not in a per-part frame. That is why an axis-aligned candidate and its negation both score well, and why the axis-alignment census alone cannot name the free axis. No candidate is free of `other` rows either, so a layout cannot be picked by "the one that is always on an axis".

## 5. Verdict

**UNKNOWN.** No candidate passes the three oracles over all 419 hinges: O1 is true for all six (it cannot choose), O2 has no input in 407/419 hinges and fails wherever it has input, and O3's parallel form fails for every candidate while its perpendicular form peaks at 151/402. Under the card's rule the constraint axis stays UNKNOWN on the fleet.

What the bytes do show, and how far it goes:

- **decoded**: the donor's five hinged panels carry a frame row that is exactly a car axis - doors vertical `[0, -+1, 0]` with the sign on the door's side, hood `[1, 0, 0]`, trunk `[-1, 0, 0]`.
- **derived**: on the five Serpente panels, `A0` (= `B0`) is the only candidate whose max-angle rotation moves the panel the way the vehicle shows (outward for doors, upward for hood/trunk), 5/5. This agrees with PHYSICS_LIMITS_REVIEW §5, which ran the same argument from the parts module's centroids.
- **UNKNOWN**: that `A0` is the axis for all 419 hinges. The fleet-wide tests available here do not reach it: the two atom frames are identical (so the engine's own second frame adds nothing), the pivots coincide (so there is no pivot-to-pivot direction), the cinfo stores one body id (so the partner is a geometric guess, not a field), and the frames are car-axis-aligned in most hinges (so alignment does not pick the column).

The experiment that would close it: pose one door at a known angle in game and read the body's rotation quaternion, then check which row stays invariant - or take a Havok 2014.1 header for `hkpLimitedHingeConstraintData`, which names the axis in the frame atom. Neither is available in this project.
