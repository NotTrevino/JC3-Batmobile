## 3. The box oracle, fleet-wide

A body is a single box when its hull fills its own bounding box: `hull volume / bbox volume >= 0.999` (a beveled or plate-like hull scores far below 1).

The three medians are equal to within 0.013, so the excess is a single scalar on the whole tensor, not a per-axis or per-body error - which is what a *different geometry* looks like and what a wrong exponent does not.

The origin model fails outright, so the stored tensor is centroidal.

## 7. UNKNOWN

1. the absolute semantics/units of the decoded diagonal: the box oracle rejects the collision-hull reading on every box body (0/107 at 1%), and the residual is a *uniform* 1.2767 factor, not scatter - so the tensor was authored from geometry the collision hull does not contain. What would settle it: the render mesh of the same part (its convex hull / tetrahedral tensor) compared under the same decode.
2. the third group (+0x20..+0x27): three lanes whose magnitudes are the largest in the block (all near +-32768) and whose own exponent lane decodes them to ~1e-36, i.e. 'no products'. Whether those lanes are products, a principal axis or a mirror tag is not decoded; the mirror test (pass 1) shows two of them flip sign between left/right parts, which both readings allow.
3. the bias 111 is a format constant measured to better than one octave (110 and 112 are 2x off); no file in this tree documents the packing, so the exact fixed-point layout of the w lane (floor(w/128) with w = exponent << 7) is derived, not documented. A Havok 2014 header for hkCompressedMassProperties would confirm it.
4. how the mass at +0x28 relates to the tensor: the box bodies show a uniform 1.275x excess, which is exactly what a 1.275x larger authoring mass *or* 1.13x larger authoring extents would produce; the two cannot be separated without the authoring mesh.
