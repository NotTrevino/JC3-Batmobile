## Answer

**REFUTED** for the candidate. `0x1400a9690` is a **constructor**, and the 8-byte
value it stores at both `+0x150` and `+0x158` is the constant **0** — a null initialiser,
not a node. The value's origin is a zeroing `xor`:

```

```

Every subsequent store to those two offsets is `mov qword ptr [...], rdx` with that same
zeroed register, so no caller's argument can reach the field. The question asked — *does a
caller chain reach a container or global that holds the spawned node* — therefore has the
answer **UNKNOWN (no such chain), with the candidate itself refuted as the node writer**.

## Why this is the right answer, and what it does not say

- It **refutes** the candidate from `RUNTIME_LAYOUT4.md` §7 rather than promoting it: the
  width oracle in that card could only see that 8 bytes are written; this card reads what
  the 8 bytes *are*.
- It does **not** find the writer of the node into `+0x150` / `+0x158`. That writer is still
  UNKNOWN, and this walk says nothing about where it is — it only removes the strongest
  image-wide candidate.
- It does **not** decide whether `+0x150` and `+0x158` are two lists (head/next) or one list
  with two anchors: both are null-initialised here and never compared.
