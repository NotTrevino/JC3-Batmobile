## 1. Answer

`RVehicleBatmobile.DriverLeft` (`Startup.upk` export 7400, 368 B script, sha1 `70471c04b9526a2616e27c3c3830155112765efb`) sets the
selector twice. Each call sits in the body of exactly one guard, and the guard's body starts at
the call's own byte (proven by the `Skip` word that lands there - section 4):

| call | value | guard token | condition bytes | condition (token-accurate) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **301** | **2** | `0x07` @248 | 251-300 | `0x82( 0x82( Core.ArmouredMode, 0x77( Core.BatmobileWinch, 0x2A ) ), Skip->301( 0x9B( Core.BatmobileWinch.WinchPullType, byte 0 ) ) )` |
| **334** | **1** | `0x07` @312 | 315-333 | `0x82( 0x81( Core.ArmouredMode ), Skip->334( Core.IsVehicleAsleep ) )` |

So **value 2 is selected by the winch/armoured condition** (`Core.ArmouredMode` combined with
`Core.BatmobileWinch` and `Core.BatmobileWinch.WinchPullType` compared against byte `0`) and
**value 1 by the `Core.ArmouredMode` / `Core.IsVehicleAsleep` condition**. The G3-01e-fix
positional guess happens to name the same two groups, but its reason was wrong: the names are
contained in the two guard *bodies*, not merely positioned before/after the calls, and the
`ArmouredMode` reference at 318 belongs to the **value-1** guard, not to the value-2 one.

The operator semantics are only partly decoded: `0x81` is a unary negation (derived from
`RPlayerController.ShowHealthBarIfNotMax`, export 98240, whose name states the negation -
`SELECTOR_POLARITY.md` section 3), `0x9B`/`0x77` are binary comparisons, `0x82` a binary boolean
operator. Which of `&&`/`||` `0x82` is, and which of `==`/`!=` `0x9B`/`0x77` are, is UNKNOWN
(section 8).

## 4. The branch words: what `Skip` proves and what `Jump` does not

### 4a. `EX_Skip` (`0x18`) - decoded, exact

A skip's 2-byte word is a **byte distance in a stream where a reference token weighs 9 bytes**,
not the 5 it occupies here: `word = (target - (at+3)) + 4 x references skipped`. Resolving it
forward in that space hits a token start in every case:

| skip at | word | references skipped | straight distance | word - distance | target |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 18 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 21 |
| 40 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 43 |
| 259 | 13 | 1 | 9 | 4 | 271 |
| 271 | 39 | 3 | 27 | 12 | 301 |
| 324 | 11 | 1 | 7 | 4 | 334 |

All 5 skips satisfy `weight_sum == word` (oracle `skip_rule_exact`), and all 12 skips in the
walkable corpus resolve the same way (oracle `skip_rule_corpus`). The two `word 0` skips at 18
and 40 are zero-length skips inside a call's argument list; they land on the next token.

This is what fixes the two guard bodies: the skip at **271** targets **301** and the skip at
**324** targets **334** - the first byte of each selector call's argument list. A skip lands on
the byte after its enclosing expression, so each call is the guarded body of the `0x07` that
owns that expression (oracle `containment_by_skip`).

### 4b. `EX_Jump` / `EX_JumpIfNot` (`0x06` / `0x07`) - UNKNOWN

The same treatment **fails** for the six jump words in this function. Every candidate base was
tested against the token starts of this very walk (`135` starts):

|---:|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | JumpIfNot | 49 | 49 **start** | 27 **start** | 71 | 49 **start** | 52 **start** | -49 | 319 | 316 **start** | 49 **start** | 49 **start** | 49 **start** |
| 30 | Jump | 81 | 81 **start** | 59 **start** | 103 | 111 **start** | 114 | -51 | 287 | 284 **start** | 81 **start** | 81 **start** | 111 **start** |
| 219 | JumpIfNot | 352 | 352 | 330 | 374 | 571 | 574 | -133 | 16 **start** | 13 | - | - | - |
| 248 | JumpIfNot | 436 | 436 | 414 | 458 | 684 | 687 | -188 | -68 | -71 | - | - | - |
| 309 | Jump | 474 | 474 | 452 | 496 | 783 | 786 | -165 | -106 | -109 | - | - | - |
| 312 | JumpIfNot | 474 | 474 | 452 | 496 | 786 | 789 | -162 | -106 | -109 | - | - | - |

The best base is `rel_pos`: 2 of 6 words land on a token start, and the two it gets are the short
entry/else words 49 and 81. That is chance level, as the corpus confirms: the same sweep over
57 jump words in 35 walkable `Startup.upk` functions gets at most 12 (`{"abs": 10, "abs_payload": 12, "abs_plus_payload": 2, "rel_pos": 8, "rel_after": 9, "rel_before": 6, "from_end": 7, "from_end_minus": 4, "expanded_abs": 10, "expanded_rel": 10, "expanded_rel_pos": 8}`): every base is at
or below the ~15% a random position would score. Single coincidences exist (the `from_end`
base of the word 352 is 16, which happens to be a token start) and are listed in the table,
but a base must resolve *every* word of a function to be the encoding, and none does.

The words are not random either: two jumps that must share a target share a word (`309`/`312`
both 474 in this function; 59/62 both 125, 116/119 both 206 in the selector), and the words
increase with the target - so they are a coordinate, just not one this script reconstructs.

A per-token-weight prefix-sum model is also impossible. With weights `bytes + r*refs + c*contexts`
each known span implies its own per-reference weight and they disagree:

* `248 -> 309`: `dw = 84` over `61` bytes, refs `5`, contexts `0`, calls `1`
* `309 -> 342`: `dw = 38` over `33` bytes, refs `2`, contexts `0`, calls `1`

* span [248, 309] implies `r = 4.6` (`+23` bytes over 5 references)
* span [309, 342] implies `r = 2.5` (`+5` bytes over 2 references)

Two different, non-integral weights for the same token kind cannot both hold, so no such model
exists (oracle `no_prefix_sum_weight_model`). Recorded as **UNKNOWN**: the two `Jump` **targets**
are not decoded, and the CFG's false-branch edges are marked UNKNOWN below. They are not needed
for section 1, which rests on the skip rule and the polarity oracle instead.
