The two sides are asymmetric, and that is the answer to the card's question. BmAK's cooked physics is **geometry only**: the four Batmobile body setups serialize no mass and no tensor, and a scale-free scan of their `AggGeom` payloads finds no triple shaped like either decoded shape's moment tensor. JC3's format *does* carry mass properties - 56 blocks in the donor - with the centre of mass proven against the hull centroid, but its inertia diagonal is not the collision shape's box or hull tensor under any of the layouts tried, so a transferable inertia value for the Batmobile cannot be read out of either side yet.

## 4. The oracle: stored diagonal vs box and hull formulas

A body counts as a single box when its hull fills its own bounding box (`hull volume / bbox volume >= 0.999`). The box formula is `m/12*(b^2+c^2)` with `m` the decoded mass and `b, c` the decoded extents; the hull formula is the exact tetrahedral tensor of the same vertices at the same mass. Both are centroidal.

| single-box bodies (`fill >= 0.999`) | 107 |

| tolerance per moment | box formula (boxes) | hull formula (boxes) | hull formula (all 1651 bodies) | of |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1% | 0 | 0 | 0 | 107 |
| 2% | 1 | 1 | 1 | 107 |

On a single-box body the hull tensor *is* the box tensor, so the middle column repeats the first by construction; the third column is the independent test (the exact hull tensor over every hull body, boxes and non-boxes alike). Both miss.


Ratio `stored / box formula` over the box bodies: p10 1.012 1.010 1.031, median **1.275 1.288 1.277**, p90 3.278 2.539 1.866. The donor's own bodies are not boxes (its best hull fills 0.773 of its bbox), so the donor can only be shown, not scored - which is why the oracle runs on the fleet.

The three medians agree to within 0.0125, so the mismatch is a single scalar on the whole tensor, not per-axis scatter: the stored tensor is consistent with a body *scaled* relative to the collision shape, which is what an authoring shape larger than the collision hull looks like. It is not evidence for a wrong exponent, which would move one axis, and not evidence for a wrong mass, which the +0x2c volume in G3-10k contradicts.

Also tested: is there a **plain-float** tensor anywhere in the donor's data section? Every 4-byte-aligned f32 triple (80571) was tested against the 8 heaviest bodies' box signatures, scale-free, at 1 %: **8 sampled hits**. 0 of the sampled offsets fall inside a `hknpShapeMassProperties` block; the rest sit in `hkGeometry` (8). A scale-free ratio test is loose - any positive triple with matching proportions passes - so these are recorded as coincidences, not read as tensors. The packed payload remains the only **located** tensor-shaped data in the file.

## 6. UNKNOWN

1. the Batmobile's mass: no mass property is serialized on any of the four BmAK body setups (decoded: their whole tag list is PreCachedPhysDataVersion, a bone NameProperty, one BoolProperty, AggGeom). Where it would be: SVehicle.ChassisMass, decoded at Research/BatmobileGrab/CLASS_DEFAULTS.md:434 as the class default 210.0 - a class default, not a per-vehicle authored value, and not this task's format.
2. the Batmobile's inertia tensor: with no mass and no tensor bytes, no box or hull formula can be evaluated on the BmAK side at all (the tool reports the formula's *shape* only). What would settle it: a decoded mass for the body plus a decision on whether the engine computes inertia from the AggGeom box (bComputedMassProperties / PrimitiveComponent field layout for the Batmobile's component), neither of which is in the files read here.
3. the absolute semantics of the JC3 stored diagonal: 0 of 107 single-box fleet bodies match m/12*(b^2+c^2) at 1% and 0 match the exact hull tensor, so the tensor is neither the collision box's nor the collision hull's. The residual is a near-uniform factor on all three moments (median 1.277), i.e. one scalar, which a wrong exponent would not produce. What would settle it: the render mesh of the same part, whose tensor at the stored mass would show whether the factor is a mass or an extent difference.
4. the third int16x4 group of the JC3 payload (+0x20..+0x27): its own exponent lane decodes its mantissas to ~1e-36, i.e. 'no products'. Whether it holds products of inertia, a principal axis, or a tag is UNKNOWN; the tool records the raw words per block.
5. the packing itself: value = mantissa * 2**(floor(w/128) - 111) is derived, not documented. The bias 111 is measured (group 1 must land on the hull centroid; 110 and 112 are a factor of two out), but no Havok header in this tree names the layout.
